CUDA out of memory on a card that looks free: read which "free" the line means

You sized the model against nvidia-smi, the card showed plenty free, and vLLM died with torch.OutOfMemoryError. A minute later nvidia-smi looks free again, so the obvious conclusion is that vLLM is misreading the card. It is not. The failure line carries three numbers, and the question is which pool "free" belongs to. There are two wordings and they mean different things.

Wording 1 — free is the device, and someone else is on it

torch.OutOfMemoryError: CUDA out of memory. Tried to allocate 768.00 MiB.
GPU 3 has a total capacity of 95.00 GiB of which 600.06 MiB is free.
Process 3617065 has 94.41 GiB memory in use.
number value what it means
total capacity 95.00 GiB the card
another process holds 94.41 GiB not yours — a different pid
free at that instant 600.06 MiB what was left
vLLM asked for 768.00 MiB more than what was left

94.41 + 0.59 ≈ 95.00: the card was fully occupied by another job when vLLM asked for 768 MiB. No flag fixes this — --gpu-memory-utilization divides memory that must be free first, and there was none. This is forum topic 1234 exactly: vLLM 0.9.0, TP 4, weights fit, one of the four cards already held by another job.

A free-looking card one minute later is not a contradiction: the message is a snapshot, and the other job may have exited in between.

Wording 2 — free is the allocator pool, and the card is fine

[rank0]: ... CUDACachingAllocator.cpp:3933 memory allocation failed with OOM on device 0
while trying to allocate 20971520 bytes (free: ... , total: ...)

Same words, different pool. Here free is what PyTorch's caching allocator had left in its own reserved segment, not what the device had. In our own corpus (39 of these lines in 2.318 s, on two ranks) 20 MiB was requested with 9.19 MiB and 10.75 MiB free, while the device summary line two lines later reports gpu.free_gib 78.46 — the card was three-quarters empty. The pool had been sized by gpu_memory_utilization × total = 0.95 × 79.14 = 75.18 GiB and was full.

So "the card has free memory" and "the allocator can allocate" are different statements, and only the second one decides whether vLLM starts. Raising --gpu-memory-utilization cannot help a card that is already shared, and it is the only thing that helps a pool that is full.

Telling them apart in one command

$ servediff show serve.log | grep torch.oom
torch.oom_device           3
torch.oom_free_mib         600.06
torch.oom_holder_gib       94.41
torch.oom_requested_mib    768.0
torch.oom_total_gib        95.0

torch.oom_holder_gib only exists for wording 1 — it is the size held by that other process. When it is present, the card is shared; when it is absent and gpu.free_gib is large, you are looking at the allocator pool. The pid from the line is deliberately not a fact: it changes every run and would make every diff say "different" for a reason that means nothing. The holder's size is stable enough to be one.

$ servediff diff run1.log run2.log
~ torch.oom_device     3 -> 2
= torch.oom_holder_gib 94.41

Same shortage, different card — a moved CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES, not a new bug.

Finding the holder

The older wording gives you the pid (Process 3617065); the newer one does not. Either way, ask the driver while the failure is happening rather than afterwards:

nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps=gpu_uuid,pid,process_name,used_memory --format=csv

Two things worth ruling out before blaming a colleague:

  • It can be your own earlier run. A killed vllm serve whose workers never exited leaves VLLM::EngineCore entries holding their slice. Re-check after stopping it, instead of reproducing and reading the same number again.
  • It can be a different physical card than you think. CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES renumbers devices, so GPU 3 in the message is the fourth visible card — often physical 7 on an 8-GPU node.

What the line cannot tell you

Not how long the other process will stay, and not whether the sharing is by design. If total comes out well under the card you bought — 79.14 GiB on a 96 GiB part — the driver has already partitioned it; check nvidia-smi -L for MIG entries before blaming vLLM.
